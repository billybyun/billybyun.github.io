Vision-Language Models (VLM)

Vision-Language Models are neural networks that jointly process images and text. They combine a vision encoder (which extracts visual features from images) with a language model (which generates or understands text), connected by a projection layer that aligns visual and linguistic representations in a shared space.

Core Architecture

  • Vision encoder – Typically a pretrained model (e.g., ViT, ResNet) that outputs image embeddings.
  • Projector – Maps vision embeddings into the language model’s embedding space (e.g., Q-Former, linear layer).
  • LLM – Receives projected image tokens + text tokens and generates output (captions, answers, actions).

Why It Matters

VLMs are central to robotics and embodied AI: they enable robots to follow natural language instructions (“pick up the red block”) by grounding language in visual perception. In BCI, similar multimodal architectures can connect neural signals to language for communication. For general ML, VLMs unlock zero-shot visual reasoning, image captioning, and visual question answering without task-specific fine-tuning.
